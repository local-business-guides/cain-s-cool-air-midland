What should an AC installation include?

Installation should begin with a review of the home, not just a quick look at the existing unit's size. The contractor should discuss rooms that stay warm, how the home is used, and whether the current system has had recurring problems. That information helps shape equipment recommendations and the work plan.

The written scope should identify the proposed equipment and describe what the crew will remove, install, connect, and test. It should also say whether the work includes adjustments to ducts, electrical connections, condensate drainage, controls, or the equipment pad. Ask the contractor to identify any work that is excluded or would need separate approval.

At the end, the installer should start the system, check its operation, and explain the thermostat and basic maintenance needs. Keep the equipment paperwork and ask who to call if the system does not cool as expected after the job.

What should a local professional check at the home?

A useful assessment considers the home's size and layout, insulation, windows, and the condition of the existing duct system. The contractor should ask about comfort problems in specific rooms instead of assuming that a larger unit will solve them. Correct sizing matters because equipment that does not match the home's needs can affect comfort and operation.

  • The condition and accessibility of the existing indoor and outdoor equipment.
  • Duct condition, airflow, and any visible gaps or damage that could affect cooling.
  • Electrical supply and whether the proposed equipment can be connected as planned.
  • The route for condensate drainage and a suitable location for the equipment.
  • Thermostat compatibility and any controls that may need to be changed.

Ask the contractor to explain what was observed and how it affects the recommendation. If the quote assumes that existing ducts or wiring can stay, have that assumption written down so there's less room for confusion once work begins.

Which installation option fits your home?

A like-for-like replacement may be straightforward when the current setup is in good condition and the new equipment suits the home's needs. A replacement that also addresses duct, electrical, drainage, or control issues takes more planning. For a new system or a change in equipment location, expect the contractor to explain the added work before you approve it.

The right option is the one supported by the assessment and a clear scope, not simply the one with the lowest equipment quote. Compare what each proposal includes, how it handles existing system issues, and what testing and handoff are part of the job.

What changes the cost of AC installation?

The price depends on more than the equipment itself. System type and capacity, removal of old equipment, access to the work area, and the amount of labor all affect the scope. Changes to ductwork, electrical connections, drainage, the thermostat, or the equipment location can add work as well.

Ask for an itemized written proposal that separates the equipment from installation work and lists any allowances or exclusions. If a contractor recommends extra work, ask what problem it addresses and whether it is required for the installation or an optional improvement. This makes it easier to compare bids on an equal basis without relying on a headline total.

How long can the work take?

Timing varies with the equipment selected, the condition of the existing system, the amount of modification required, and the crew's schedule. A direct replacement can involve a different amount of work than a project that needs duct changes, new wiring, or a different equipment location. Equipment availability and any required approvals can also affect when work can begin.

Before booking, ask for the expected start date, how long the crew expects to be on site, and what might change that estimate. If the home will be without cooling during the work, ask how the crew will communicate delays and when they expect to restore operation.

What installation mistakes should you avoid?

  • Choosing a system based only on the size or model of the old equipment.
  • Accepting a quote that does not describe the equipment, included work, and exclusions.
  • Assuming ducts, wiring, or drainage will be suitable without asking how they were checked.
  • Approving extra work without a clear explanation and updated written scope.
  • Skipping the final operating check or leaving without understanding the system handoff.

Call a professional if the system has repeated cooling problems, the proposed equipment seems oversized or undersized without an explanation, or the quote leaves important work unclear. Also pause before approving a plan that changes electrical connections or equipment placement without explaining how those details will be handled.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What did you check in my home before recommending this equipment?
  • How did you decide what system capacity and type fit the home's layout and cooling needs?
  • What equipment, removal, installation, testing, and cleanup are included in the written scope?
  • Are duct, electrical, drainage, thermostat, or location changes expected, and are they included?
  • What could lead to added work, and will you get my approval before doing it?
  • Who will complete the installation, and who should I contact if something does not operate as expected?
  • Do local permit or inspection requirements apply to this particular project, and who will confirm that?
  • What is the expected schedule, and what could delay the work?

Local requirements can depend on the type and extent of work. Ask the contractor to explain what applies to your project and verify any open questions with the local building department before work starts.
