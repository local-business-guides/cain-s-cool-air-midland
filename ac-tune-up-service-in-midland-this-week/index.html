What should an AC tune-up include?

A tune-up is a planned inspection and maintenance visit, not a promise that every existing problem will be fixed as part of the appointment. Ask the provider to describe the work included before you book, since the scope can differ from one service call to another.

  • Check the thermostat settings and the system's basic operation.
  • Inspect accessible electrical connections and components for visible concerns.
  • Check the indoor and outdoor equipment, including airflow and drainage where accessible.
  • Look for signs of wear, unusual noise, leaks, or other conditions that may need a separate repair.
  • Explain any findings and get your approval before adding repair work.

Ask whether the visit covers both indoor and outdoor equipment, and whether filter replacement or cleaning is included or billed separately. If the technician finds a fault, request a clear explanation of the recommended repair and its cost before authorizing it.

What can change the cost of a tune-up?

There is no single price that applies to every home and system, and no price is listed here. The amount quoted can depend on what the provider includes, the equipment being serviced, how many systems need attention, and whether the visit is routine maintenance or a response to a problem.

Extra cleaning, replacement parts, troubleshooting, or repairs may be separate from the tune-up charge. Ask for the service fee and the included tasks up front, then ask how the technician will price added work and whether you can decline it before anything begins.

What should you check before the technician arrives?

You can make the visit smoother without opening equipment or attempting electrical or refrigerant work. Note when the issue happens, any unusual sounds or smells, and whether the system has been cooling consistently.

  • Confirm the appointment window and the service address.
  • Make sure the technician can reach the thermostat and the indoor and outdoor units.
  • Have the system model information available if it is easy to find.
  • Tell the provider about any current symptoms before the visit.
  • Ask whether there is anything the provider wants you to do before arrival.

What mistakes should you avoid?

Do not assume that every listing called a tune-up includes the same inspection or cleaning. A vague promise of a low fee can leave out important work, so get the scope and any possible extra charges explained before you agree.

Avoid treating routine maintenance as a substitute for a needed repair. If the system is making a new noise, leaking, shutting off, or failing to cool, describe those symptoms when you book and ask whether the appointment should be handled as troubleshooting instead.

When should you call a professional instead of waiting?

Call an HVAC professional if the system will not run, is not cooling, repeatedly shuts off, or shows signs of water around the equipment. If you notice burning smells, smoke, or a suspected electrical problem, stop using the system if it is safe to do so and seek prompt professional guidance.

A tune-up appointment may not be the right service for an active fault, so explain what is happening before the provider schedules the visit. That helps the company determine whether you need maintenance, diagnosis, or repair.
