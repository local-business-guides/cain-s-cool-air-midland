What should an AC tune-up include?

An AC tune-up is preventive maintenance, not a promise that an aging or damaged system will work like new. The exact checklist depends on the equipment and the company, so ask what the quoted visit includes before scheduling.

A typical appointment involves checking system operation, looking for wear or obvious problems, and cleaning or inspecting accessible components as appropriate. If the technician finds a repair issue, ask for a separate explanation and estimate rather than assuming it is part of the tune-up.

What will the technician look at?

The technician should consider the system as a whole, not just one part. A useful visit includes checking how the equipment starts and runs, looking for signs of damage or unusual wear, and discussing anything that could affect reliable operation.

  • The indoor and outdoor equipment, including visible condition and access for inspection.
  • Airflow and the filter's condition. Ask if a replacement filter is included or billed separately.
  • Electrical connections and controls, where those checks are part of the provider's stated scope.
  • Drainage and visible signs of water around the indoor equipment.
  • The system's operating performance and any noises or symptoms you have noticed.

This list is a starting point, not a universal checklist. Ask the company to explain which checks apply to your system and whether cleaning, replacement parts, or diagnostic work would be an additional charge.

What affects the cost of an AC tune-up?

There is no single price that fits every system or appointment. The quote can depend on equipment type, access, the work included, and whether the visit is strictly preventive maintenance or also involves diagnosing a reported problem.

Ask whether the quoted amount covers inspection, cleaning, a filter, or other routine materials. Also ask how the provider will price repairs, replacement parts, or extra labor if something needs attention. A clear estimate separates the scheduled tune-up from any work that requires your approval.

If two quotes differ, compare their written scopes instead of looking at the total alone. One may include more tasks, while another may charge separately for them. Ask for the difference in plain terms before choosing.

When should you schedule, and how long might it take?

Booking before you urgently need cooling gives you more room to compare schedules and address any findings without the pressure of a breakdown. If the system is already struggling, mention that when you call. The company may need to book a diagnostic or repair visit rather than treating it as a routine tune-up.

Appointment length depends on the equipment, access, and the work included, so get a time estimate from the provider rather than relying on a general rule. Let them know if the outdoor unit is difficult to reach, the indoor equipment is in a tight space, or there are symptoms to investigate.

What mistakes should you avoid?

A tune-up should give you useful information, not pressure to approve work you do not understand. Keep the conversation focused on the system's condition and the reason for each recommendation.

  • Do not assume every company uses the same checklist. Ask for the scope before booking.
  • Do not treat a repair recommendation as automatically included in maintenance. Request a separate explanation and estimate.
  • Do not choose by the lowest quote alone. Check whether the same tasks, materials, and follow-up are included.
  • Do not ignore a new noise, weak airflow, or water around the equipment. Describe the symptom when you schedule.
  • Do not approve work you cannot connect to a specific finding. Ask what the technician observed and what could happen if you wait.

Questions to ask before you hire: checklist

A few direct questions can help you compare providers and avoid surprises on the day of the visit. Write down the answers so you can compare the same details across calls.

  • What inspection and maintenance tasks are included in the tune-up?
  • Are cleaning, filters, or other materials included, or charged separately?
  • Is the appointment maintenance only, or can it include troubleshooting?
  • How will you explain and price repairs before doing additional work?
  • What information about my system do you need before giving a quote?
  • How long should I set aside, and what access should I prepare?
  • If you recommend a repair, will I receive the findings and estimate before deciding?

What local rules should you check?

Routine maintenance and equipment changes are different kinds of work, and local requirements can depend on the job. If a tune-up leads to a repair, replacement, or other work beyond inspection and maintenance, ask the contractor whether permits or other local requirements apply.

You can also check with the relevant local authority if you want to confirm requirements independently. Do not assume a maintenance appointment includes permits or approvals for separate work.
